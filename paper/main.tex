\begin{abstract}
Edge semantic segmentation under adverse visual conditions must account for more than clean-image accuracy. It must also respect the latency, compiler, memory, and energy behavior of the target accelerator. We present \method, a compiler-portable elastic segmentation framework that trains one restricted-operator supernet and extracts static subnets for heterogeneous edge backends. The current evidence covers Cityscapes and ACDC evaluation, four elasticity levels, TensorRT GPU execution, and Hailo-8 execution. At 100k training steps, mIoU increases monotonically with model size across clean and adverse conditions; the large level reaches 0.4712 on Cityscapes and 0.4919, 0.3312, 0.4517, and 0.4492 on ACDC fog, night, rain, and snow, respectively. A first apples-to-apples augmented comparison against Fast-SCNN passes the predefined parity gate, with the supernet winning four of five splits by 0.3--1.3 mIoU points and losing ACDC/rain by 1.2 points. Measured end-to-end latency ranges from 3.714 to 41.14 ms on Hailo-8 and from 0.912 to 9.389 ms on an AGX Xavier TensorRT GPU. Under an identical 10 ms budget, the best level is device-dependent: small on Hailo-8 and large on the TensorRT GPU. Quantization-aware training, energy measurement, calibrated risk routing, and the remaining ablations are not yet complete and are intentionally not claimed here.
\end{abstract}
