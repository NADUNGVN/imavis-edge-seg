\title{Supplementary Material for PACE-Seg: When Does Input-Adaptive Semantic Segmentation Pay Off on Edge Accelerators?}
\author{D\~ung Nguy\~{\^e}n}
\begin{abstract}
This supplement records checkpoint provenance, the measurement protocol, Hailo-8 sensitivity configurations, compiled-engine accuracy, calibration quality, condition-level routing results, and additional qualitative examples for the main manuscript. All numbers come from the same landscape-input training runs (seeds 0--2) and measurements as the manuscript; no new claims or evaluation samples are introduced.
\end{abstract}
\end{frontmatter}

\section{Evidence scope}

The main manuscript reports three findings: a FLOPs-proportional proxy does not reproduce measured feasibility; weight sharing costs the largest capacity 0.9--2.8 \miou{} points relative to training it alone; and, once every route and every static deployment is timed in the same harness, routing under a hard budget beats rank escalation but not static deployment, whose advantage would vanish under mean-cost budgets only below a break-even overhead of about 1.3~ms (AGX Xavier) and 4.3~ms (Hailo-8). This supplement contains the material needed to reproduce and audit these results. The 120 operating cells are correlated evaluations, and the two devices share segmentation predictions.

\section{Training and checkpoint provenance}

All models are trained at the native 2:1 aspect ratio of Cityscapes and ACDC, with input sizes $384\times192$, $512\times256$, $768\times384$, and $1024\times512$ (width$\times$height) for tiny to large. Elastic training uses the sandwich rule with boundary-aware cross-entropy and in-place distillation from the largest level ($\alpha=0.5$, $T=1$); 100,000 steps, batch size 8, AdamW (learning rate $3\times10^{-4}$, weight decay $10^{-4}$), 500 warm-up steps with cosine decay, gradient clipping at 5.0, and FP16 mixed precision. The references for RQ2, Fast-SCNN and the large architecture trained alone (PACE-Large), use the same recipe and three seeds each. Table~\ref{tab:provenance} lists the supernet checkpoints used by every router analysis.

Candidate-only latency (RQ1) uses the trtexec/hailortcli protocol at batch size one on all four devices. Complete-route cost $C_h(\ell)$ and static cost $S_h(\ell)$ are measured on AGX Xavier and Hailo-8 in one process, with the same engines, inputs, and timer boundary: the timer starts when a preprocessed host tensor is handed to the runtime and stops when the returned prediction is synchronized; decoding, resizing, and normalization are excluded. A route runs the tiny engine, entropy, and the policy decision and, unless tiny is selected, one further engine. Each configuration uses 50 warm-up and 500 timed iterations; the median, p95, and p99 are recorded. Cold-start costs are excluded.

Figure~\ref{fig:route-cost-expansion} compares candidate-only p95 latency with the complete-route median. On Hailo-8 the short tiny route expands by $11.0\times$ because network-group activation and host-side entropy dominate it.

\section{Router protocol and leakage safeguards}

Segmentation quality is dataset-level \miou{}, computed once from the accumulated confusion matrices of the selected candidates. Each validation split is divided by alternating index into fit and held-out halves. Calibrators, risk targets, T-hard thresholds, and operating points use only fit halves; held-out labels are read only after selection. Five risk targets are the 10th--90th percentiles of fit-half observed error, macro-aggregated across splits, with one grid per run shared by both devices. A cell is violating if at least one held-out image is routed to a candidate whose tabulated cost exceeds the budget; fair cells for comparisons with policy A are those in which A has no violation.

\section{Calibration quality}

Table~\ref{tab:calibration} reports held-out calibration quality per split. Figure~\ref{fig:reliability} shows reliability pooled over all splits. All four calibrators lie close to the diagonal; replacing ten equal-count bins by five bins or isotonic regression changes the held-out error by at most 0.003, whereas twenty bins overfit the small ACDC fit halves.
