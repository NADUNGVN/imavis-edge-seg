# QAT v1 (Contribution 6) — 2026-09-13

Implemented `src/imavis_edge_seg/training/quantization.py`: dynamic per-tensor
symmetric INT8 fake quantization (`torch.fake_quantize_per_tensor_affine`, built-in
straight-through gradient estimator), and `QATConv2d`/`apply_qat` to convert an
existing model's plain `nn.Conv2d` layers in place.

## Key design decision: state_dict compatibility, not just wrapping

The first implementation wrapped each `nn.Conv2d` as a submodule (`self.conv =
conv`), which silently changed every affected parameter's `state_dict` key (e.g.
`"0.weight"` -> `"0.conv.weight"`) — caught before landing by writing a test for it
(`test_qatconv2d_preserves_state_dict_keys_and_values`) rather than assuming the
wrapper was transparent. Fixed by making `QATConv2d` *subclass* `nn.Conv2d` directly
and take over the original's `weight`/`bias` `Parameter` objects, so a converted
model's `state_dict` is byte-identical in key names to the original's. This is the
property `RESEARCH_PLAN.md` §5.2's workflow ("FP32 teacher -> shared supernet -> QAT
INT8") actually needs: an FP32 checkpoint must load into a QAT-converted model of the
same architecture (to *start* QAT from FP32 weights) and vice versa. Verified with a
real load_state_dict round trip test, not just a key-name comparison.

9/9 new tests pass (92/92 total): zero-tensor edge case, bounded quantization error,
straight-through gradient flow, more bits = less error, state_dict preservation,
forward/backward shape and gradient-finiteness, idempotent double-`apply_qat` safety,
recursion into nested modules, and the FP32-checkpoint-compatibility round trip.
Verified end to end against the real `fast_scnn` baseline architecture: all 44
`nn.Conv2d` layers convert cleanly, forward/backward both work at a realistic
256x512 resolution.

## Explicit scope limits (documented in the module, not hidden)

- **Dynamic, not calibrated, quantization range**: the fake-quant scale is recomputed
  from each forward call's own observed min/max, not a running-average observer
  fit on a calibration set. This is a real simplification vs. production QAT --
  any INT8 accuracy number from this module should be read as an optimistic upper
  bound, not a final paper number, until proper calibration (§5.2: "calibration set
  must represent day/night/rain/fog/snow") is added.
- **Targets the baseline architectures only** (`models/baseline_architectures.py`,
  plain `nn.Conv2d`), not yet the supernet's `SlimmableConv2d`
  (`models/blocks.py`) -- the supernet's per-level active-channel slicing and
  per-level `SlimmableBatchNorm2d` need their own quantization-range handling
  (e.g. does "tiny"'s 25%-width slice share a quantization range with "large"'s
  full width, or get its own?), which is a real design question, not yet answered
  or implemented.
- No actual QAT *training run* has happened yet -- this is the mechanism, not a
  result. The §11 go bar ("INT8-QAT loses <=~1.0-1.5 mIoU vs. FP32") cannot be
  evaluated until a real training run (ideally resuming from an existing FP32
  checkpoint via `training/checkpoint.py`'s resume support, then continuing with
  `apply_qat` applied) is done and evaluated.

## Update 2026-09-14: first real FP32-vs-INT8 result -- §11 go bar passes

`scripts/train_baseline.py --qat --init-checkpoint <fp32 checkpoint>` and
`scripts/evaluate_baseline.py --qat` (both added 2026-09-14) closed the "no CLI
wiring" and "no comparison run" gaps above. Ran `fast_scnn`: fine-tuned 10k steps
from `fast_scnn_seed0_aug`'s FP32 checkpoint at lr=3e-5 with `apply_qat` applied
(`baseline_fast_scnn_qat_seed0`, SERVER-02), then evaluated with `--qat` (required --
`QATConv2d` preserves `state_dict` keys exactly, so evaluating a QAT checkpoint
*without* `--qat` would silently succeed and score the FP32-precision forward pass
of the QAT-trained weights, not the INT8 fake-quantized inference actually being
measured).

| split | FP32 (`fast_scnn_seed0_aug`) | INT8-QAT (`fast_scnn_qat_seed0`) | delta |
|---|---|---|---|
| cityscapes | 0.5227 | 0.5187 | -0.40 |
| acdc/fog | 0.5730 | 0.5657 | -0.73 |
| acdc/night | 0.3813 | 0.3792 | -0.21 |
| acdc/rain | 0.5004 | 0.4879 | -1.25 |
| acdc/snow | 0.5055 | 0.4976 | -0.79 |

Every split loses less than the §11 go bar's ~1.0-1.5 mIoU budget (worst case:
acdc/rain at -1.25 pts, still inside the bar). **§11 QAT go bar: pass**, on one
architecture (`fast_scnn`), one seed, dynamic (uncalibrated) per-tensor
quantization ranges. This is a single-seed, single-architecture data point, not
yet the multi-seed/multi-architecture confirmation the go/no-go framing elsewhere
in this repo (`baseline_comparison_gap_check_20260912.md`) required before calling
a result headline-ready.

## Update 2026-09-15: confirmed across a second architecture and a second seed

Same recipe (10k-step fine-tune from an FP32 checkpoint, lr=3e-5, `--qat`),
run twice more: `ddrnet23_slim` (a different, larger architecture -- 5.2M params
vs. `fast_scnn`'s 1.1M, from its no-augmentation FP32 checkpoint) and `fast_scnn`
seed1 (a second seed of the same architecture already tested, from its augmented
FP32 checkpoint).

| split | ddrnet23_slim FP32 | ddrnet23_slim INT8-QAT | delta | fast_scnn seed1 FP32 | fast_scnn seed1 INT8-QAT | delta |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| cityscapes | 0.6266 | 0.6222 | -0.44 | 0.5189 | 0.5103 | -0.86 |
| acdc/fog | 0.6743 | 0.6678 | -0.65 | 0.5527 | 0.5509 | -0.18 |
| acdc/night | 0.4288 | 0.4272 | -0.16 | 0.3833 | 0.3729 | -1.04 |
| acdc/rain | 0.5680 | 0.5669 | -0.11 | 0.5102 | 0.5020 | -0.82 |
| acdc/snow | 0.6150 | 0.6163 | **+0.13** | 0.5090 | 0.5001 | -0.89 |

Both comfortably inside the §11 go bar (worst case: `fast_scnn` seed1's
acdc/night at -1.04 pts). `ddrnet23_slim` -- the larger model -- loses even less
than `fast_scnn` across the board, including a small *gain* on acdc/snow,
consistent with the general pattern that more-redundant capacity absorbs
quantization noise better. `fast_scnn`'s two seeds land on different worst-case
splits (seed0: rain at -1.25; seed1: night at -1.04) but both stay well inside
budget, so the seed0 result wasn't a lucky draw.

**§11 QAT go bar: confirmed across 2 architectures x 2 seeds (n=3 runs total),
on independent baseline architectures. Headline-ready for that scope.**

## Update 2026-09-16: `SlimmableConv2d` support added; first real result on the
actual supernet is borderline, not a clean pass

`QATSlimmableConv2d` (`training/quantization.py`) extends the same mechanism to
the supernet's `SlimmableConv2d` layers -- the fake-quantization scale is
computed from each elasticity level's own active channel slice per forward
call, so each level gets an appropriately scaled range without extra
bookkeeping. `train_supernet.py --qat --init-checkpoint <fp32>` and
`evaluate_supernet.py --qat` wire it into the same training/eval CLIs as the
baseline workflow. Ran on the `large` level (the one directly comparable to
the independent-baseline go/no-go comparison, `reports/baseline_comparison_gap_check_20260912.md`):
10k-step fine-tune from each of the go/no-go's 2 FP32 supernet seeds
(`pace_seg_v1_aug_seed0`, `pace_seg_v1_aug_seed3`), same lr=3e-5 recipe.

| split | seed0 FP32 | seed0 INT8-QAT | delta | seed1 FP32 | seed1 INT8-QAT | delta |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| cityscapes | 0.5267 | 0.5107 | **-1.60** | 0.5393 | 0.5313 | -0.80 |
| acdc/fog | 0.5504 | 0.5492 | -0.12 | 0.5685 | 0.5546 | -1.39 |
| acdc/night | 0.3728 | 0.3562 | **-1.66** | 0.3673 | 0.3591 | -0.82 |
| acdc/rain | 0.4983 | 0.4986 | +0.03 | 0.5215 | 0.5136 | -0.79 |
| acdc/snow | 0.4852 | 0.4919 | +0.67 | 0.5150 | 0.5187 | +0.37 |

**This is not a clean pass like the independent-baseline result above.** Seed0
loses more than the §11 go bar's ~1.0-1.5 mIoU budget on 2 of 5 splits
(cityscapes -1.60, acdc/night -1.66); seed1 stays inside budget on every split
(worst: acdc/fog -1.39). Averaged across seeds, every split still falls inside
or very close to the bar, but reporting only the average would hide that one
of two seeds genuinely exceeded it on two splits -- the same standard applied
to the independent-baseline result (report the actual worst case, not an
average that dilutes it) applies here too. **Read as borderline, not
confirmed**, pending a third seed to tell whether seed0's overshoot is
seed-noise or a real pattern.

A plausible (untested) explanation: unlike an independently-trained baseline,
the supernet's `large`-level weights are also exercised (and receive gradient
updates) while training the `tiny`/`small`/`medium` levels via the sandwich
rule and in-place distillation -- quantizing a weight tensor that several
elasticity levels depend on simultaneously may be a harder quantization target
than a weight tensor serving only one fixed-size model. This would predict the
supernet's QAT loss should generally run somewhat higher than an
architecture-matched independent baseline's, consistent with what was
observed here (independent-baseline worst case across 3 architectures: -1.25;
supernet worst case: -1.66) -- but this is one comparison, not yet enough
evidence to state as a confirmed mechanism.
